Abstract

We present TeensyVAD (TeensyVAD by VoxLogic), a family of voice activity detectors sized 20,449–99,593 parameters — small enough to read end-to-end in one sitting, implemented in pure NumPy with hand-written backpropagation, and native to 8 kHz telephony audio (no resampling anywhere). Across four training generations we study what matters at this scale: (1) labels — distilling frame targets from Silero VAD tightens onset/offset boundaries by 2–3× over synthetic construction labels; (2) data — capacity only pays when data scales with it (families are flat 20k→100k on 1M frames, but improve monotonically on the 37.9M frames of the full 100 h LibriSpeech train-clean-100); and (3) quantization — quantization-aware training recovers accuracy over post-training quantization at identical size, yielding a 28 KB int8 model that outperforms its 87 KB float32 parent. On human-labelled real-world audio — the TEN VAD public set and AMI SDM meetings — the best 100-hour model reaches 0.880 ROC-AUC on TEN (FlashVAD v0.1 publishes 0.882 with 2.3× the parameters) and AMI frame F1 0.886 vs Silero's stock 0.714 (the 1.77M-parameter teacher's default threshold misses 44% of speech in real rooms). Offline batched inference runs at 0.064 µs/frame with ONNX Runtime int8 — the fastest figure we are aware of among open VADs in this mode, enabled by the stateless-MLP design (recurrent VADs cannot batch through time). Everything — code, weights, evaluation harness, and this site's charts — is reproducible from the linked repositories.

The family

TeensyVAD-v1 — the 20k baseline

A complete VAD in 20,449 parameters (87 KB), pure NumPy, hand-written backprop, native 8 kHz — the construction-labels baseline that beats the energy VAD by 0.44 event F1 (0.976 vs 0.535) and shows capacity alone buys nothing on 1M frames.

LibriSpeech dev-clean + ESC-50 · synthetic F1 0.903 · AMI F1 0.887 (calibrated) · paper · HF repo

TeensyVAD-v2 — Silero distillation

Same architecture, Silero-teacher labels: the teacher disagreed with construction labels on 11.6% of frames; the student tightens onsets from −432 ms to −172 ms and matches FlashVAD's published TEN F1 (0.890 vs 0.889) with 2.3× fewer parameters.

~1M distilled frames · TEN F1 0.890 (best thr) · 28 KB QAT int8 · paper · HF repo

TeensyVAD-v3 — scaled data, real-room hardened

10× the data (10.7M frames, ~30 h) with 7-talker babble and real AMI room ambience at SNR −5…20 dB; first generation evaluated on human-labelled audio. Capacity pays at last: the sweet spot moves to 80k (TEN AUC 0.873 → 0.877).

TEN AUC 0.873→0.877 · AMI F1 0.886 vs Silero 0.714 · domain threshold profiles · paper · HF repo

TeensyVAD-v4 — the 100-hour generation

All 28,539 utterances of LibriSpeech train-clean-100 (37.9M frames), the full 20k–100k sweep plus a QAT int8 ladder. v4-80k posts the family's best real-world AUC (0.880 ≈ FlashVAD's 0.882 at 2.3× fewer params); the 28 KB QAT beats its 87 KB float parent.

TEN AUC 0.880 (v4-80k) · best AMI AUC 0.863 (80k QAT) · paper · HF repo
