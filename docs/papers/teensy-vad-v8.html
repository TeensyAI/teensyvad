Abstract

We present teensy-vad-v8, the eighth generation of the teensyvad family — a 500k-parameter scaling ablation. The v7 champion, a 42k-parameter single-layer GRU, beats every larger model in family history, which raises the obvious question: was 42k simply too small a budget for scale to pay, or does scale itself not fit this problem? v8 answers it by training four ~500k models — 549k–582k parameters, 13–14× the champion — under an identical, upgraded recipe: 660 h of prior-balanced LibriSpeech+MUSAN mixtures, Silero distillation, and quantization-aware fine-tuning. The answer is negative but informative: no v8 model dominates the champion. Deep nets win real rooms (deep F1 0.919–0.923) but collapse on clean near-mic ranking (TEN AUC 0.73–0.78 vs 0.89); the context-MLP scales best, and its int8 QAT variant matches the champion's TEN AUC at family-best TEN F1 0.9133 — with its artifact 4× smaller than the float version. v7-GRU96 remains the shipping champion; v8 is a research release. Weights are CC BY 4.0 on MUSAN-safe data.

1 · Introduction

v7 closed the capacity debate inside the MLP family: a single-layer GRU of 96 units (41,809 parameters, 169 KB) distilled from Silero beats the entire v5/v6 MLP family on all four real-world metrics (TEN F1 0.8992, TEN AUC 0.8934, AMI F1 0.9153, AMI AUC 0.9182) at 37.9 µs per 20 ms chunk. But a 42k model winning against everything larger leaves the scaling question open: the v5 ablation peaked at 80k on 100 h of data with 100 ms of context — maybe a 42k recurrent model is a local optimum that a genuinely larger model, given more data, better-balanced data, and modern training, would pass. v8 is that test: push the budget to ~500k parameters (roughly where the 1.77M-parameter Silero teacher becomes reachable) while upgrading everything else at once — 6.6× the training data (660 h), prior-balanced mixture construction, and QAT fine-tuning — across the three architecture families the project has used (recurrent, attention, stateless-with-context).

2 · Method

Four ~500k models are trained under one recipe. (i) v8-GRU-192×3: a 3-layer GRU with 192 units per layer in a residual-free stack (582k parameters) — the v7 recurrence hypothesis scaled up. (ii) v8-transformer d128×4: a causal transformer, 4 layers, 8 heads, dmodel 128, over a 2.5 s window (570k parameters). (iii/iv) v8-a3 context-MLP, ctx 25 frames (250 ms) with 448/224 hidden units (549k parameters), trained in float and fine-tuned to int8 with QAT (554 KB → 4× smaller than the 2,157 KB float artifact). All four share the family feature front-end (8 kHz mono, 25 ms frames / 10 ms hop, 20 log-mel filters + deltas) and are trained on 660 h of prior-balanced LibriSpeech+MUSAN mixtures (speech prior 0.72 vs the natural 0.91) with Silero distillation, each finished with 2 epochs of QAT fine-tuning. Inference uses the family's pure-NumPy streaming runtimes; all timings are the full streaming path per 20 ms chunk.

4 · Findings

(1) Scale did not beat fit. No v8 model dominates the 42k champion. Deep nets win AMI rooms (F1 0.919–0.923) but collapse on clean near-mic ranking (TEN AUC 0.73–0.78 vs 0.89). (2) The context-MLP scaled best. a3-int8 matches v7's TEN AUC with family-best TEN F1 0.9133 at 13× size. (3) QAT won. int8 fine-tuning improved TEN ranking (0.8844→0.8949); QAT is validated at 500k. (4) Recurrence > attention at every tested scale for clean-audio ranking. (5) Honest caveat: only ~1.8 training passes over 660 h — under-training is a confound; scale may still win with proportional compute. v7-GRU96 remains the shipping champion.

5 · Ablations & Corrections

Correction to the v7 record. The v7 documentation attributed the 360 h failure to speech-frame prior imbalance; the root cause was later found to be a filename-sort bug in the labeling pipeline (lexicographic ordering scrambles utterances past #10,000 with %05d names). The prior-balanced padding fix shipped in v8 remains beneficial (0.72 speech prior vs 0.91), but the 360 h numbers themselves were artifacts of scrambled labels.

Capacity ceiling, recapped. The v5 ablation showed TEN AUC peaking at 80k and falling past it; v6 showed a wider window beats width; v7 showed memory beats both. v8 extends the picture: even at ~500k parameters, 660 h, and QAT, extra capacity still does not dominate a well-fit 42k recurrent model — the architecture/data fit, not the parameter count, remains the binding constraint at telephony scale.

6 · Limitations

Under-training is a confound: the four v8 models saw only ~1.8 training passes over 660 h, so the negative scaling result may partly reflect budget, not scale itself — scale may still win with proportional compute. The int8 NumPy streaming runtime is unoptimized (281.9 µs vs 75.0 µs float); int8's speed case awaits real int8 BLAS kernels, though its artifact is 4× smaller. Training is English-only (LibriSpeech read speech). And Silero keeps the clean near-mic crown: the 1.77M-parameter teacher still ranks best by TEN AUC (0.9514), so none of the family — at any scale — yet contests clean near-mic ranking.
