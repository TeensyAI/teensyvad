Abstract

We present teensy-vad-v6, the sixth generation of the teensyvad family, built around one measured finding: at 100 ms of temporal context, capacity has a ceiling — and it is low. A controlled scaling ablation over the v5 family shows TEN VAD ROC-AUC peaking at 80k parameters (0.8877) and falling as the model grows (100k 0.8865, 150k 0.8827, 200k 0.8849) while latency climbs 64→75 µs. Instead of adding parameters, v6 widens the model's temporal context from 100 ms to 250 ms (plus one hard-example-mining variant) and changes nothing else. Every v6 point at ≤49k parameters sits above the entire v5 100 ms capacity curve on TEN F1: v6-a1 (24,441 params) reaches TEN F1 0.9081, and v6-a2 (49,249 params) posts TEN AUC 0.8870 and the family's best AMI AUC (0.8726) with 60% fewer parameters than the v5-80k peak — at identical speed (64–66 µs per 20 ms chunk). Silero still leads clean near-mic ranking (TEN AUC 0.9519, recurrent, 36× the parameters); teensy leads real rooms (AMI F1 0.88+ vs 0.714). Weights are CC BY 4.0 on the same MUSAN-safe data as v5.

1 · Introduction

v4 showed capacity paying all the way to 100k on validation F1; v5's 80k set the family record (TEN AUC 0.8877). The natural next move was more parameters — so we measured where that stops paying, and the answer was: almost immediately. Scaling the v5 family 20k→200k (all at 100 ms context) peaks at 80k and degrades beyond it, even with 37.9M training frames. The failure is not optimization or overfitting of labels; the 100 ms input window simply saturates — extra capacity is spent re-describing context the model already has, at a 19% latency cost for the largest size. v6 tests the orthogonal axis: if the window is the bottleneck, widen the window, not the weights. It is — at 24k parameters, 250 ms of context beats the family's entire measured 100 ms capacity curve on TEN F1.

3 · Method

Identical to v5 in every respect except two levers. (i) Context widening: the stacked input window grows from 10 frames (100 ms) to 25 frames (250 ms) of the same 40-dim frame features, so the MLP input goes 400-dim → 1000-dim — the v6-a1/a2 variants (k25). (ii) Hard-example mining (v6-c): training oversamples frames where Silero-teacher and construction labels disagree, at the standard 100 ms context and unchanged size. Architecture, features, data, calibration and runtime are otherwise exactly the v5 recipe: 8 kHz mono, 25 ms frames / 10 ms hop, 20 log-mel filters (80–3800 Hz) with band-mean subtraction plus deltas, ReLU MLP with sigmoid output, hysteresis + hangover streaming with AMI-calibrated thresholds. Exports: float .npz and ONNX float32/int8 (26K–204K npz / 22K–50K int8 ONNX per variant).

5 · Limitations

English speech only; no music in training (music reads as "activity"). Context is 250 ms — wider than the family's 100 ms, but not unbounded: recurrent teachers like Silero still hold the clean near-mic crown (TEN AUC 0.9519), and v6 does not close that gap. No acoustic echo cancellation — use an upstream echo canceller (e.g. Asterisk's). At distant-room operating points, false-alarm rates remain high on overlapped meeting speech for all systems (labels mark foreground speech only).
