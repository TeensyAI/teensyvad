Abstract

We present teensy-vad-v7, the seventh generation of the teensyvad family — and its first recurrent model. The v6 capacity ablation showed that at fixed 100 ms context, more parameters hurt: the v5 family peaks at 80k (TEN AUC 0.8877) and falls past it, and even v6's widened 250 ms window is still a stateless summary. v7's finding is that recurrent memory was the missing ingredient. Replacing the MLP with a single-layer GRU of 96 units (41,809 parameters, 169 KB npz), distilled from Silero VAD with chunked backpropagation through time, beats the entire v5/v6 MLP family on all four real-world metrics: TEN F1 0.8992, TEN AUC 0.8934, AMI F1 0.9153, AMI AUC 0.9182. At 37.9 µs per 20 ms streaming chunk, it is also the fastest model in family history (vs 63–75 µs for the MLPs). Against the 1.77M-parameter Silero teacher, v7 wins both AMI room metrics (F1 0.9153 vs 0.7136; AUC 0.9182 vs 0.8938); Silero keeps the clean near-mic crown (TEN AUC 0.9519). Weights are CC BY 4.0 on MUSAN-safe data.

1 · Introduction

Two generations of evidence pointed the same way. v5's controlled capacity ablation (20k→200k parameters, all at 100 ms context, 37.9M training frames) showed TEN AUC peaking at 80k (0.8877) and falling past it — 100k 0.8865, 150k 0.8827, 200k 0.8849 — while latency climbed 64→75 µs: capacity has a ceiling, and it is low. v6 attacked the orthogonal axis and widened the input window from 100 ms to 250 ms, putting every v6 point at ≤49k parameters above the entire v5 curve — but the wider window is still a fixed, stateless summary of the past. The hypothesis for v7: what the family lacked was not width, window, or parameters, but memory — a learned, compressible state that can carry speech/nonspeech evidence across an unbounded past the way the 1.77M-parameter teacher already does. v7 tests that hypothesis by changing exactly one thing: the stateless MLP becomes a recurrent GRU.

2 · Method

The feature front-end is exactly the family recipe: 8 kHz mono, 25 ms frames / 10 ms hop, 20 log-mel filters (80–3800 Hz) with band-mean subtraction plus first-order deltas (40 dims/frame). The model is a single-layer GRU with 96 units (41,809 parameters total) followed by a sigmoid projection — the family's first recurrent architecture. Training is in PyTorch, distilling Silero soft labels frame-by-frame over the same MUSAN-safe mixtures as v5/v6, with chunked backpropagation through time in 2.5 s chunks (state carried across chunks at inference). The shipped runtime is the family's pure-NumPy streaming engine with hand-written GRU recurrence; its output is verified against the PyTorch reference to 1.8e-07 max absolute error. Exports: float .npz (169 KB) with thresholds in JSON metadata; streaming decisions run through the family's hysteresis + hangover stage with AMI-calibrated thresholds.

4 · Ablations and negative results

Two documented negatives frame the design. (i) Capacity ceiling (v5 family, 100 ms context): growing 80k→200k parameters lowered TEN AUC 0.8877→0.8849 at a +19% latency cost (64→75 µs) — more parameters hurt past 80k, which is why v7 adds memory, not width. (ii) Naive data scaling: scaling the training mixtures to 360 h with the natural 91% speech-frame prior lowered TEN AUC to 0.8586 — the class-prior shift swamps any data benefit. Prior-balanced mixtures are future work; v7 trains on the v5/v6 prior-balanced recipe unchanged.

5 · Limitations

English speech only; no music in training (music reads as "activity"). Silero keeps the clean near-mic crown: the teacher still ranks best by TEN AUC (0.9519 vs v7's 0.8934), so v7's wins are real rooms (both AMI metrics) and speed, not clean near-mic ranking. There is no acoustic echo cancellation — use an upstream echo canceller (e.g. Asterisk's). At distant-room operating points, false-alarm rates remain high on overlapped meeting speech for all systems (labels mark foreground speech only). As a recurrent model, v7 cannot batch through time for offline inference — the family's 0.064 µs/frame batched figure remains an MLP-only result.
